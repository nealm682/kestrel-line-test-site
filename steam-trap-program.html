Our steam trap program

How we survey, replace, monitor and maintain steam traps in occupied hospitals and federal facilities. This is the same program we ran for 1,140 traps at the Dallas VA Medical Center from 2023 to 2025.

KL SteamWatch wireless monitoring

  • Monitoring runs on our own platform, KL SteamWatch. Every trap gets a battery-powered acoustic and surface-temperature sensor that reports through Kestrel Line-owned LTE cellular gateways. Nothing connects to the customer's network, so no government IT authorization is needed.
  • Each sensor reports every 15 minutes. Trap statuses: operating, failed open, failed closed (cold), leaking, and sensor offline.
  • When a trap's status changes, the platform generates an alert, timestamped to the second, and automatically emails and texts the COR and our steam services manager. The system logs the time the alert was generated, the time the COR notification was sent, delivery confirmation, technician acknowledgment and repair close-out.
  • Sensor uptime on the Dallas VA job: 99.2% of trap-hours reporting.

Unedited data exports

Raw machine exports are available at any time in CSV and JSON. Exports are generated by the system, and our staff cannot edit them.

  • Reading log: one row per reading per trap, with the trap tag ID, location, timestamp, status, acoustic level (dB), inlet and outlet temperature, and battery level.
  • Alert log: one row per alert, with the alert-generated time, COR-notification time, acknowledgment time and close-out time.
  • The COR gets a read-only account on the customer portal to pull exports directly.
  • At the Dallas VA Medical Center we delivered monthly unedited exports for every trap, including alert-generation and COR-notification timestamps. The COR used them to check our invoices.

Crew, subcontracting and backup coverage

  • Typical crew for a 600 to 1,200 trap program: 1 foreman, 4 journeyman steam technicians and 2 apprentices during replacement; 2 technicians for ongoing preventive maintenance and repair.
  • No subcontractors on steam trap programs. Controls programming, the only trade we ever subcontract, isn't needed because KL SteamWatch is our own system.
  • Backup: 10 more steam-qualified technicians in Dallas/Mesquite and San Antonio can be on site within 24 hours to cover leave, surges or emergencies.

Transition in and out

  • Before sensors are live on a trap, we check it with handheld ultrasonic rounds every two weeks, and failed-open traps are replaced or isolated within 3 working days.
  • At contract end we run a 30-day handoff: full trap inventory, every historical export, the sensor and gateway list, and a spare-parts list go to the customer or the next contractor. Sensors and gateways become the customer's property at no added cost.
